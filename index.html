How the score works

Every food gets a score out of 10, built from three parts:

Nutrition – up to 6 points. Based on the product's Nutri-Score grade: A gets 6, B gets 4.8, C gets 3.3, D gets 1.8 and E gets 0.6.

Additives – up to 3 points. Start at 3, then take off 0.25 for each yellow additive, 0.75 for each amber one and 1.5 for each red one (it can't go below zero). Green additives cost nothing.

Processing – up to 1 point. Based on the NOVA group: unprocessed (1) gets 1, processed ingredients (2) get 0.8, processed foods (3) get 0.5 and ultra-processed foods (4) get 0.

Amounts matter: labels don't show how much of each additive is used, so for additives with a safe daily limit (ADI) the app estimates the worst case – the legal maximum in one serving – and compares it with the limit for your body weight. If that could be half your daily limit or more, the additive's colour goes up one level.

Safety caps: if a product has any red additive, or a Nutri-Score of E, its score can't go above 4.9.

Non-food products (cosmetics, household) have no nutrition data, so they start at 10 and lose 0.5, 1.5 or 3 points for each yellow, amber or red ingredient.

Ratings draw on EFSA, IARC and UK FSA positions. Many links (e.g. emulsifiers and cancer) come from observational studies, which show association, not proof of harm.

Where the data comes from

Each scan checks these in order until one has the product:

1–4. Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts, Open Products Facts and Open Pet Food Facts – free crowd-sourced databases with millions of products, best for the UK and Europe.

5. USDA FoodData Central – US government database of branded foods. It has no Nutri-Score, so the nutrition part is estimated from fat, sugar and salt levels (drinks are judged on sugar, like Nutri-Score does).

6. UPCitemdb – general product names and photos only, so no score can be given.

When a product has no additive list, additives are picked out of the ingredient text by E-number and name.

Product data from Open Food Facts (ODbL) and the sources above – entries can be incomplete or wrong.
